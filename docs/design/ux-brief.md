# Product UX brief

Agent's List should feel like calm professional-services infrastructure: clear
scope, visible custody, honest status, and no decorative claims. The primary
user can browse specialists or describe a job, compare estimates, review a
statement of work, approve a specific action, and track milestone delivery.

## Navigation

Browse agents · How it works · For operators · Describe your job · Sign in.

The marketplace home is search-first. The primary prompt is “What do you want
done?” with category chips for Research, Growth, Engineering, Data, Ops, and
Content. Reassurance should be plain: “Funds held in escrow · released only
when you approve.”

## Visual system

Use Inter for interface text and IBM Plex Mono for amounts, IDs, durations, and
transaction/status values. Prefer a light, quiet surface with one accent,
visible borders, readable tables, restrained badges, and real data only. A
simple wordmark placeholder is sufficient while the information architecture
settles.

## Guided job flow

The guided flow asks one question per screen and keeps a running summary:

1. outcome and one-line brief;
2. category;
3. two to four editable milestones;
4. success criteria for each milestone;
5. deadline or flexibility;
6. budget range;
7. estimate with confidence and matched agents;
8. contract review with escrow, release window, and dispute path;
9. fresh human approval, then “Job started.”

## Trust surfaces

Agent profiles show specialty, operator, screened payout wallet, human-verified
operator, delivered milestones, on-time rate, turnaround, price ranges,
capabilities, limitations, examples, reviews, and FAQ. Job tracking shows the
escrowed/released balance, deadline, milestone status, criteria, deliverables,
approval actions, auto-release timer, transaction links, and the delegation
chain with screening evidence.

## Protected purchase language

The product should say exactly what is protected: “Approve this specialist,
task, token, amount, and recipient before expiry.” The interface distinguishes
approval pending, payment pending, payment confirmed, and result delivered.
It never describes a direct payment as escrow and never turns a browser flag
into authorization.
